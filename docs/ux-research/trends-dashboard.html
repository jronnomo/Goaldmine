Goaldmine · /trends — Trends Dashboard

Pixel mockup · 2 Sep 2026 · chosen direction: One Instrument, Honest Denominator · every panel must be inspected in both themes and in grayscale

What this is. Seven 390px frames rendering the chosen /trends IA with the real globals.css token block. It is a specification artifact, not a prototype: nothing fetches, nothing animates, and the only script is the two toggles. Every series is generated — the weight curve, the weigh-in mask, the 7-day mean, the calorie marks, the three macro bands and every rail cell were computed by gen_trends.py from the report's formulas, so the geometry is exact and reproducible rather than eyeballed.

The one idea. One <Card>, three panels, one x-axis rendered exactly once, and underneath it the rail — two 8px availability lanes that show which days actually carried data behind every average on the page. Excluding unlogged days is the correct math; Song & Szafir showed that silent exclusion still scores worst on perceived data quality, so absence is shown twice: as a hole in the series and as a mark on the rail. The honest denominator is the feature.

★ The grayscale toggle is load-bearing, not decoration. This palette is iso-luminant — --accent↔--target is 1.16:1, --accent↔--warning is 1.01:1, and --muted↔--success is 1.00:1, byte-identical in grayscale. The acceptance test for every identity decision here is that a grayscale screenshot loses nothing. Press the button on every panel. Panel 7 is that test made permanent.

Two hard failures are already known and are not hidden. --border on --card is 1.59:1 light / 1.39:1 dark and fails 3:1 — so the rail's absent state is a hole, not an encoding, and the coverage sentence is load-bearing rather than redundant. And --danger in dark is 3.38:1 and fails AA text — which is one of the reasons a deficit never renders red. --danger (#A82A1F / #C0392B) appears nowhere in this artifact. Note also that --target is the rust #9A480F / #D97A3D, not the barn-red a stale profile file lists.

Every height on this page is arithmetic, not measurement. Nothing here has been rendered on a device. The footer table lists every committed value with its range. The single most urgent check is the fold: the page bottoms at exactly 737px with ZERO clearance.

One Card, three panels, one axis. Panels are separated by border-t border-[var(--border)] hairlines and the x tick labels render on the bottom of the stack only. Three separate Cards would cost ⚠[120–180px] of card walls and would visually deny the axis-sharing that is the entire point.

Heights are the signed-off budget — weight h-48 192 · calories h-32 128 · macros h-10 40. ⚑ These differ from the PRD's 208 / 176 / 160, which overruns the fold by ⚠[93–193px] and pushes the rail below it — forfeiting the two ideas (caption-echo, live gate) that make this direction better than the PRD's.

The unlogged day renders no mark at all. 29 of the 90 calorie slots are empty by construction. No ghost bar, no hatched bar, no zero — a mark where there is no datum is zero-filling in a costume. Absence gets its mark on the rail instead, whose axis is availability.

The weight mean breaks, it does not bridge. The 7-day trailing mean emits a point only when ≥3 readings sit in the window, so the 14-day silence at Jun 25 – Jul 8 produces exactly two polylines, not one line drawn straight through a hole. 48 raw dots sit under it at r=2, alpha 0.70 — see the footer on why not 0.55.

★ The fold is drawn honestly. The dashed rule is at exactly 737px and the caption's third line bottoms on it. ⚠ Clearance is ZERO, not the ⚠[0–15px] §3.1 first claimed. The arithmetic: shell 16 + hero 49 + gap 16 + chips 44 + gap 16 = 141; Card = 1 + 16 + 205 + 1 + 156 + 1 + 95 + 15 + 44 + 45 + 16 + 1 = 596; total 737. If a real device measures the AppHeader or BottomNav even 4px taller than the house constants, the caption clips. Pre-authorised first concession: calories h-32 → h-28.

Coverage sits above the averages, not below them. In the window panel the coverage line 61 of 90 days logged · 48 weigh-ins renders first. Coverage after an average is a footnote nobody reads; coverage before it is a frame.

Balance is --foreground with an explicit −. Never --danger: a deficit is not an error, and --danger in dark measures 3.38:1 and fails AA text anyway.

Set dates is a peer, not a fallback — it sits directly beneath the rail caption, where a user who has just failed to discover the drag is already looking, rather than buried at the bottom of the page. ⚠ It must never contain a chart: a ResponsiveContainer inside a closed <details> measures 0×0.

The committed window is a DOMAIN CHANGE, not a highlight. Excluded data is gone, not dimmed — so there is no selection band in this state at all. Compare panel 3, where the band exists because the domain has not changed yet.

At 27.2 px/day the ribbon becomes ten fat columns and the var(--card) separators finally do visible work. This is the panel that proves the separator is a fix, not an ornament.

The track carries an --accent segment with --foreground 2px end-caps, the shipped CeilingRule "stile" idiom, because an --accent-soft fill has no edge and the window's boundaries are the fact being communicated.

Calories may still render; macros cannot. kcal and the macro columns are separate nullable fields on the same row, so a quick-logged day is a real logged day with a hole in it. Those days are excluded from the macro averages, not zeroed.

What survives. The fixed band order (P bottom, C middle, F top, never re-sorted by magnitude) · the var(--card) separators, which are a hard boundary rather than a hue difference · the in-place P/C/F labels at the right edge · the dashed plan-target line, whose discriminators are the dash and the horizontality, not its hue · and the silhouette of the calorie marks with its 29 holes.

What does not. All three macro hues collapse. --target↔ --success is 1.05:1 and --accent↔--target is 1.16:1; in grayscale the three bands are three barely-different greys. That is the correct outcome, because hue was only ever tertiary reinforcement here. If you can still read the ribbon — and you can — the encoding was never carried by colour.

⚑2 This is why the macro hues moved off the blueprint's placeholder. That set put --accent and --warning — 1.01:1, the palette's single worst pair — on the two non-adjacent bands, where a separator cannot help. The shipped P/C/F convention was adopted instead; its worst adjacent pair is target↔success at 1.05:1 with a hard card-stroke boundary between them.

The grams ⇄ % toggle changes the SCALE, never the ENCODING. Same fixed order, same separators, same labels in both modes. A toggle that re-encodes teaches the reader that the picture is arbitrary.

What this artifact does NOT settle

  • Any height. All of it is arithmetic. The fold at exactly 737px with zero clearance is the check that gates the whole direction, and it can only be answered on hardware.
  • Whether the rail reads as a rail rather than as a second x-axis. Two 8px lanes sitting 15px under a real x-axis is a genuine risk and no amount of arithmetic answers it.
  • Whether the 11px lane labels and the 11px in-place P/C/F labels survive the real Geist metrics. Every text width in the 40px gutter and the 14px right margin was computed against a system stand-in face.
  • The gap sign convention for the Apple Health row (not drawn here — hasHealthData is false for every user today). Pin it in trends-core with a unit test: if implementation flips it, every sentence of the ledger copy inverts silently and reads as confidently wrong.
  • Whether the shipped WeightChart chip and dot defects get fixed in the same PR. Both are shipped a11y failures that /trends would otherwise copy forward; fixing them changes an existing surface, so it is a scope decision.
